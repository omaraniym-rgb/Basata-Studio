# Basata 2.0 — Six-Month Workplan

**Positioning & offer first · 20 August 2026 → 19 February 2027**
*Owner: Omar Yusuf (Omarani) · Client work: winding down gradually*

---

## 0. What this plan is for

Six months to answer one question with evidence, not conviction:

> **Is there a paying audience for what you know about simplicity — and what exactly do you sell them?**

Everything else — the book, the community, the movement — is downstream of that answer. This plan deliberately does **not** start with writing the book. A book written before the thesis is tested is a book that sells to nobody. The book is a *gate at month six*, not a task at month one.

The plan also assumes you are not free. Client work is still funding this, and that constraint is built into the weekly rhythm rather than wished away.

An honest inventory, from the archive:

Banks (Salaam Bank), telecoms (Hormuud, Astaan), government (OPM Somalia, Office of the Attorney General, NEA, MoEWR, Ministry of Agriculture), health (Kaafi Hospital, Star Healthcare), fintech (S1Pay, Toospay), plus a long tail of SMEs and startups.
- **Government identity work at national scale.** FGS visual identity refresh, Brand Somalia, PCG org charts, ministry logo crisis material. This is rare. Very few people anywhere have done national brand identity work in a post-conflict state.
- **A functioning practice's worth of internal IP** — proposals, scopes of work, brand strategy blueprints, templates, a business plan, board minutes.
- **The Basata 2.0 logo**, already made.
- **Omarani** as a personal mark, and a podcast that already existed in 2023.
- **The word itself.** *Basata* (بساطة) — simplicity. The name already carries the thesis in a language most of your competitors don't speak.

**What you do not have**

- Any written articulation of what "ultimate simplicity" actually *means* as a method. The Basata 2.0 folder contains one file: a logo.
- A public body of work under your own name. The portfolio is client-owned, scattered, and mostly unpublished.
- An audience list you own. No newsletter, no email list, nothing you control.
- Evidence anyone will pay you for teaching rather than doing.
- A tested price point for anything that isn't a service engagement.

**The honest risk**

"Simplicity" as a design position is crowded and, on its own, unsellable — every studio on earth claims it. Dieter Rams, John Maeda, and a hundred Medium posts got there first. Your defensible ground is almost certainly **not** simplicity in the abstract. It's simplicity *under specific hard conditions*: low-trust markets, institutions with no design maturity, government clients, multilingual and multi-script contexts, teams with no budget, countries rebuilding their public image from scratch. That's a position nobody in the Western design-thinking canon can occupy.

Phase 1 of this plan exists to test that hypothesis rather than assume it. **Do not fall in love with "simplicity" before you find out which part of it people actually want.**

## 3. The six-month sequence

### Phase 0 — Excavate (Mon 24 Aug → Fri 11 Sep, 3 weeks)

*Goal: get the thesis out of your head and out of the archive, before you name anything.*

You cannot position from memory. The material is in 150 project folders. Mine it.

**Do**

1. **Select 20 candidate projects** from Client Work 2023–2026. Bias toward: (a) work you're proud of, (b) work that failed instructively, (c) government and institutional work, (d) work where the *simple* answer was the hard-won one.
2. **Write a one-page teardown for each.** Fixed template, no exceptions:
   - The situation in three sentences
   - What everyone assumed the answer was
   - What you actually did
   - What got removed, and what it cost to remove it
   - What happened
   - The transferable principle
3. **Clear rights as you go.** Some of this is government work under NDA or procurement terms. Build a one-line status per project: *public / needs permission / never publishable*. Do this in week one — it constrains everything downstream and it's the kind of thing that ambushes you in month four.
4. **Extract the patterns.** After 20 teardowns, sit with them and ask: what do I keep doing? What do I keep refusing to do? What do clients always fight me on? That list is the raw ore of the method.
5. **Interview 8 past clients.** 30 minutes each, recorded. Ask what changed for them, in their words. Do not ask if they liked the work. Ask what it let them do. Their language is your marketing copy — you cannot invent it.

**Produce**

- 20 one-page teardowns
- A rights-status sheet
- A raw pattern list (unpolished, long, contradictory — that's fine)
- 8 client interview recordings + transcripts

**Time budget:** ~15 hrs/week. This is the cheapest, highest-leverage work in the whole plan.

### 🚦 Gate 1 — Fri 11 Sep

Read the pattern list cold. **Is there a real, specific, non-obvious idea in there?**

- **Yes** → proceed to Phase 1.
- **It's just "make things simple"** → you don't have a thesis yet. Extend Phase 0 by two weeks and go deeper into the *failures*, which is where the specific thinking always hides.

---

### Phase 1 — Define (Mon 14 Sep → Fri 16 Oct, 5 weeks)

*Goal: a defensible position, a named method, and a first read on whether anyone cares.*

**Do**

1. **Write the positioning statement.** One page. It must survive this test: *could a competent competitor say the same sentence?* If yes, it's not positioning, it's a mood. Include explicitly:
   - Who it's for (be uncomfortably narrow — e.g. "institutions in emerging and post-conflict markets that must earn public trust," not "brands")
   - What you claim that others don't
   - What you're deliberately against
   - What you refuse to do
2. **Name and define the method.** 5–7 principles, each with a name, a one-line rule, and — crucially — a real example from a teardown. Principles without cases are aphorisms. Aphorisms don't sell.
3. **Test the position on 10 people before publishing a word of it.** Split them: 4 past clients, 3 peers who'll be blunt, 3 people in your target audience who don't know you. Ask them to explain your position back to you. If they can't, it's not clear yet. Iterate.
4. **Settle the naming question.** Basata? Omarani? Both? Decide now, in writing, with reasoning — it determines every URL, handle, and cover you buy afterwards. My read: *Basata* is the movement and the idea; *Omarani* is the person who teaches it. But decide it deliberately, once.
5. **Build the minimum publishing surface.** One page explaining the position. One email signup. Nothing else. No portfolio site, no full rebrand, no CMS. **You do not need a website in month two.** A single page and a list is enough to run the next phase, and building more is the most seductive form of procrastination available to a designer.

**Produce**

- Positioning statement (1 page, dated)
- The method: name + 5–7 defined principles
- Naming decision memo
- One live page + email capture

**Time budget:** ~12 hrs/week.

---

### 🚦 Gate 2 — Fri 16 Oct

**Can three strangers read your page and correctly explain what you stand for and who it's for?**

- **Yes** → proceed to Phase 2.
- **No** → do not start publishing. Nothing downstream fixes a position people can't repeat. Loop back for two weeks.

---

### Phase 2 — Teach in public (Mon 19 Oct → Fri 11 Dec, 8 weeks)

*Goal: convert the archive into public teaching, build the list, and find out what actually lands.*

This is the phase that generates the evidence everything else rests on. It is also the phase where the pivot becomes visible and irreversible-feeling. Expect that to be uncomfortable.

**Do**

1. **Publish one teardown per week, for eight weeks.** Same template, cleaned up, visuals from the archive. These are your proof, your teaching material, and — later — your book chapters. You are writing the book; you're just not calling it that yet.
2. **Send a weekly email.** Same cadence, same day. The teardown plus one short piece of thinking. Consistency matters more than length; a 400-word email that arrives every Tuesday beats a brilliant one that arrives whenever.
3. **Post daily-ish on one platform. Exactly one.** Pick where your audience actually is — likely LinkedIn for the institutional/government side, X for the regional design scene. Do not run four channels. Running one well for eight weeks tells you more than running four badly.
4. **Teach live, three times.** A free 60-minute session on one principle, repeated monthly (late Oct, mid Nov, early Dec). Record all three. This does four things at once: builds the list, forces clarity, tests whether people will *show up*, and gives you the raw material for a paid workshop later. Live teaching exposes weak thinking faster than any amount of writing.
5. **Start the community — small and closed.** After session one, invite the 20–30 most engaged people into one group. Not a public server. A room. Give it one purpose and one weekly ritual. **Movements start as rooms, not as launches.** A public community with 12 lurkers is worse than no community.
6. **Track what lands.** Weekly: which teardown got read and shared, which principle people quote back, which questions repeat. The repeated question is your product.

**Produce**

- 8 published teardowns
- 8 weekly emails, list at 300–500
- 3 recorded live sessions
- A 20–30 person core group
- A signals log: what resonated, what died

**Time budget:** ~15 hrs/week. This is the heaviest phase. Plan client work around it, not the reverse.

---

### 🚦 Gate 3 — Fri 11 Dec

Three questions:

1. Is the list growing without you begging? (Target: 300+, growing weekly.)
2. Did people show up live and come back? (Target: 40+ registered by session three, 30% returning.)
3. Is there a question people keep asking that you could charge to answer?

**Two or more yes** → proceed to Phase 3.
**Fewer** → the position is right but the audience or channel is wrong. Change the channel or narrow the audience before changing the idea. Don't rewrite the thesis on eight weeks of data.

---

### Phase 3 — Package and charge (Mon 14 Dec → Fri 19 Feb 2027, 10 weeks)

*Goal: first revenue that isn't a client service, and a decision on the book.*

Note the holiday dip: weeks of 21 Dec and 28 Dec are light. Plan for eight working weeks, not ten.

**Do**

1. **Build one paid offer. One.** Choose the shape from the Phase 2 signals log, not from preference:
   - *Cohort workshop* — 2–3 sessions, 15–25 people, $150–400/seat. Best if live sessions were your strongest signal.
   - *Self-paced course* — the method plus teardowns. Better margins, needs a bigger list.
   - *Advisory/diagnostic* — a fixed-scope, fixed-price engagement applying the method. Highest price, closest to what you already do — which is both the safety and the trap.
   - *Paid membership* — only if the core group is genuinely alive and generating its own conversation.
2. **Sell it before you build it.** Announce, take deposits or a waitlist with intent, and only then build the material. If 15 people won't commit to a $200 workshop from a 400-person list, the offer is wrong and you've learned it for the price of an email instead of six weeks.
3. **Run it once, in January.** Deliver imperfectly and on time. Collect testimonials and, more importantly, collect what confused people.
4. **Decide on the book — with evidence.** By mid-February you'll have 12+ teardowns, a defined method, transcripts from three live sessions, and paying customers. That is a book's skeleton and its proof of demand. The decision to make is scope and route:
   - Self-publish fast, as an audience asset, or pursue a publisher for institutional credibility
   - What the book *is*: the method with cases (most likely), a memoir of building a studio in Somalia, or a field manual for institutions
   - Write it in 2027 as a defined project — **not** squeezed into these six months
5. **Set the wind-down endpoint.** By February you'll know what 2.0 earns. Set the date and terms for the last client engagement.

**Produce**

- One paid offer, sold and delivered once
- First non-service revenue
- 4 more teardowns (12 total)
- Book decision memo: format, scope, route, 2027 timeline
- Client-work exit date

**Time budget:** ~15 hrs/week.

## 4. The parallel track: winding down Basata Studio

This runs alongside everything above. It is not a phase; it's a constant.

**Principles**

- **Cash first.** Do not accelerate the wind-down out of enthusiasm. The pivot's greatest enemy is a cash panic in month four, which will drive you straight back into client work at exactly the wrong moment.
- **Change what you accept, not how hard you work.** From September, apply a filter to every new inquiry: *does this project produce a teardown, a case, or a testimonial for 2.0?* If not, decline it or price it high enough that it buys you two months of writing time.
- **Raise prices instead of raising volume.** Fewer, better-paid engagements free the calendar. This also road-tests whether your new positioning commands a premium.
- **Finish clean.** The 2026 clients — Hormuud, Al Bidaya, Surad, Xaraash, Kulan, Rajo Party — are your last references and your best sources of testimonials and permissions. Close them well.

**Milestones**

| Date | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| Fri 11 Sep | Client-work filter written; applied to all new inquiries |
| Fri 16 Oct | Team/subcontractor conversations held — who continues, who doesn't |
| Fri 11 Dec | No new engagements accepted past March 2027 |
| Fri 19 Feb | Wind-down endpoint set in writing; final engagements scheduled |

**Also handle:** the business licence and tax registration (BASATA TCC) — decide whether the legal entity continues under 2.0 or is wound up, and confirm what obligations follow either way. Get this checked with an accountant rather than guessed; it is not urgent in month one, but it should not surface as a surprise in month five.

## 5. Weekly operating rhythm

The whole plan fits into a fixed week. Protect the shape, flex the content.

| Day | Block |
|-----|-------|
| **Mon** | Client work. All of it, front-loaded. |
| **Tue AM** | Deep writing — teardown or essay. No meetings, ever. |
| **Tue PM** | Publish + send the email. |
| **Wed** | Client work. |
| **Thu AM** | Deep writing / building the offer. |
| **Thu PM** | Conversations — client interviews, community, live sessions. |
| **Fri AM** | Client work. |
| **Fri PM** | Review: what landed, what's next, update the signals log. 45 minutes, non-negotiable. |

Two protected deep-work mornings a week is roughly 200 hours across six months. That is enough to build everything in this plan — *if it's protected*. The single biggest predictor of whether this pivot works is whether Tuesday and Thursday mornings survive contact with a client emergency.

**Monthly (first Friday):** review the six success metrics. Write two paragraphs on what you learned. Keep them; they're the memoir.

## 6. What not to do in these six months

Explicitly out of scope. Each of these is a real temptation and each one kills a positioning phase:

- ❌ **Write the book.** The teardowns *are* the book, gathered without pressure. Deciding to write it in month one commits you to a thesis you haven't tested.
- ❌ **Rebrand Basata 2.0 properly.** You have a logo. That is enough for six months. As a brand designer this will itch. Resist it — a beautiful identity for an untested position is the most expensive kind of procrastination.
- ❌ **Build a big website.** One page, one signup.
- ❌ **Launch a public community.** Rooms first. Servers later, if ever.
- ❌ **Run four social channels.** One, well.
- ❌ **Take a large client project because it's flattering.** In months 3–5 this will show up dressed as an opportunity.
- ❌ **Announce the movement.** Movements are recognised, not declared. Publish the work; let people name it.

## 7. Open questions to resolve early

These are unresolved and each one changes the plan. Answer them in Phase 0 and 1, not later.

1. **What's the runway?** How many months can you cover if client income halved? This number sets the risk tolerance for everything and it isn't in the plan yet because only you know it.
2. **Language.** Somali, English, or both? This is not a marketing detail — it decides who the movement is *for*. A simplicity movement taught in Somali for the Horn is a genuinely different (and possibly more powerful) thing than an English one aimed at the global design audience.
3. **Geography.** Is the audience regional (Somalia, Horn, East Africa, Gulf) or global? Regional is smaller but you are unambiguously the leading practitioner. Global is bigger and you're one voice among thousands.
4. **Who's in the room?** Are the students designers, or the institutional leaders who hire designers? Very different products, very different prices.
5. **What happens to the team?** The board minutes and team CVs suggest others depend on this. Their futures need addressing by Phase 1, not month five.
6. **What are you actually good at that isn't design?** The archive shows a great deal of communications strategy, proposal writing, and institutional advisory. The teaching business may be broader than "design simplicity."

## 8. If it isn't working

Honest exit ramps, so you're deciding by evidence rather than by mood:

- **If Gate 1 fails twice** — the thesis isn't there yet. Keep the studio, keep publishing teardowns as marketing, revisit in six months. No shame in this; it's the cheapest possible finding.
- **If Gate 2 fails twice** — the idea may be real but you haven't found its language. Consider working with an editor or a sharp outside reader. This is a very common and very fixable failure.
- **If Gate 3 fails** — audience or channel problem, not an idea problem. Change one variable, run another eight weeks.
- **If Phase 3 produces no sales** — you have an audience but not a customer. Either the offer is wrong (fixable, try a second shape) or the audience can't pay (harder — reconsider who it's for).
- **The genuine warning sign** is none of the above. It's month four with no teardowns published, the website half-rebuilt, and a new client project that ate September. If you find yourself there, the problem isn't the plan — it's that Tuesday and Thursday mornings stopped existing.
